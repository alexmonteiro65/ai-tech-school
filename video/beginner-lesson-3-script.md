## English

Hi, I'm AITS, your AI Tech School guide — here's the detailed walkthrough for this lesson. You've made it to the last lesson of the Beginner path, and it answers a question that trips a lot of people up: what is an API, and why do you actually need one?

API stands for Application Programming Interface — a defined way for one piece of software to ask another for something, and get a response back. Here's the analogy that makes it click. Think of a restaurant. You don't walk into the kitchen and cook your own meal — you order from a menu, and a waiter carries your order to the kitchen and brings the result back. The menu and the waiter are the API: a fixed, reliable way to ask for something without needing to know how the kitchen works inside.

That gives you two ways to use Claude. Claude.ai, the chat website, is great for talking to Claude directly, one conversation at a time. The Claude API lets your own software send requests to Claude directly, so Claude can be built into an app, a website, or an automation — not just a chat window.

Here's why that matters: without an API, Claude only lives inside a chat window. With an API, your own product can ask Claude questions and use the answers automatically — which is exactly what Claude Code and MCP, coming up next in the Intermediate path, are built on.

On the page, you'll trace what actually happens, step by step, when an app talks to Claude through the API, then finish with three quick questions — and with that, you'll have completed the entire Beginner path. Nicely done. I'll see you in Intermediate.

Before you try the quiz below, here's a quick preview of the three questions and their answers. Question 1: In the restaurant analogy, what does the API represent? Is it The food, The menu and waiter connecting you to the kitchen, The customer, or The building? The answer is The menu and waiter connecting you to the kitchen — The API is the fixed, reliable way to ask for something without needing to know how the kitchen (Claude's model) works inside. Question 2: What's the main limitation of only using Claude.ai, the chat website? Is it It costs more, Claude can't live inside your own app or automation, It's slower, or It doesn't support English? The answer is Claude can't live inside your own app or automation — Claude.ai is one conversation at a time in a browser — the API is what lets Claude be built into other software. Question 3: What happens right after your app sends a request to the API? Is it Nothing, requests are one-way, The API processes it and sends back a response, You have to restart the app, or Claude calls you? The answer is The API processes it and sends back a response — It's a request-response cycle: your app asks, the API (and Claude) answers, and your app receives that answer.
