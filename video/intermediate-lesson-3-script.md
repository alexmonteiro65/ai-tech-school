## English

Hi, I'm AITS, your AI Tech School guide — here's the detailed walkthrough for this lesson. Last lesson of the Intermediate path — let's talk about automation.

Automation with Claude Code means describing a task once, then letting Claude carry out the steps — with your review — instead of you doing them by hand every single time. Here's a real example: "Every time I add a new blog post file, generate an SEO-friendly meta description and add it to the top of the file." Claude Code can read the new file, draft the description, and edit the file — all inside your project.

Not every task is a good candidate. The good ones share three traits: repetitive — you'd otherwise do this the same way, often. Well-defined — there's a clear right shape for the result. And verifiable — you can check quickly whether it worked.

Every automation breaks down into the same three parts: a Trigger, what starts the task. An Action, what Claude actually does. And a Check, how you verify it worked. One rule worth remembering: keep a review step early on. High-stakes, irreversible actions with no check are bad automation candidates — start with something you can verify quickly.

On the page, you'll design a real automation yourself, then check your understanding with three quick questions — and that completes the entire Intermediate path. See you in Expert.

Before you try the quiz below, here's a quick preview of the three questions and their answers. Question 1: What makes a task a good candidate for automation? Is it It's high-stakes and irreversible, It's repetitive, well-defined, and easy to verify, It only happens once, or It requires no review, ever? The answer is It's repetitive, well-defined, and easy to verify — Repetitive, well-defined, and verifiable tasks are the safest and most valuable to automate first. Question 2: In the Trigger, Action, Check model, what is the Check step for? Is it Starting the automation, Verifying the result actually worked, Deleting the task, or Billing? The answer is Verifying the result actually worked — Check is how you confirm the automation actually did what you expected before you trust it fully. Question 3: Why keep a review step in early automations? Is it It's required by law, It catches mistakes before they become real problems, It makes Claude respond slower for no reason, or It has no real benefit? The answer is It catches mistakes before they become real problems — A review step catches mistakes while the stakes are still low — you can loosen it later once you trust the automation.
