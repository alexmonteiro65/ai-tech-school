Building AI agents that work autonomously

Autonomy is powerful and risky in equal measure. This lesson is about designing it responsibly, not just enabling it.

1

Explanation

An agent is an AI system that loops: observe (gather information), think (decide what to do), act (take an action, often via a tool), then repeat — until the goal is done, largely without a human approving every single step.

That's the key difference from Claude Code's default mode, where you review each step. An agent is trusted to make a chain of decisions on its own, within guardrails you define up front.

The real risk

Autonomy means mistakes can compound before a human notices. Good agent design always includes:

  • Clear boundaries — exactly what it's allowed to touch
  • Observability — logs of what it actually did
  • A stopping condition — a limit or a point where it must hand off to a human
Example: an agent that monitors a support inbox and drafts replies — but only sends automatically for the cases it's confident about, and escalates everything else to a human.
